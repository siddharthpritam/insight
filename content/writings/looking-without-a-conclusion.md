When a familiar feeling appears, we often recognise it before we have really looked. We call it irritation, worry, or disappointment. A name arrives, followed by a story about why it is here and what we ought to do with it.

Naming has its place. It helps us speak to one another and make sense of what happens. Yet a familiar name can also bring an inquiry to an end. We feel that we know, and move on.

Perhaps we can stay a little longer with the experience itself. There is a sensation in the body, a thought repeating, an impulse to speak or turn away. We need not immediately arrange these things into an explanation. We can notice how they arise, how they affect one another, and whether they remain the same when we give them our attention.

This does not require a special state of mind. The wish to understand, the impatience, and the effort to get somewhere are also part of what is happening. They too can be noticed.

The inquiry begins wherever we are: in a conversation, while walking, or in a quiet moment alone. We may discover something unexpected. We may simply become more aware of how quickly we reach for a conclusion.

For now, it may be enough to leave a little room between what we experience and what we believe we already know.
